Science · Stress response · Immediate + extended 01 / 05

The brain under stress — immediately, and for as long as the stressor lasts.

Two phases, two timescales. Acute stress floods the PFC with catecholamines (Arnsten 2009) and reconfigures whole-brain networks via noradrenaline from the locus coeruleus within seconds (Hermans 2011 Science). Extended stress — the weeks of sustained elevation that characterize a multi-month drawdown — retracts PFC dendrites in rodents at 3 weeks (Radley 2004: −20% apical length), and measurably degrades attention, working memory, and DLPFC connectivity in humans (Liston 2009). The central, counter-intuitive point: the subjective sense of stress habituates after a few exposures. The biological impact does not.
Medical residents: 4 weeks of sustained exam stress disrupts DLPFC connectivity AND attention-shifting; 4 weeks after stress ends, both recover fully.

Attention-shift cost (ms): elevated in Session 1 (p<0.01), normalized by Session 2 (NS). C. Functional connectivity from left & right DLPFC: reduced in Session 1, restored in Session 2.

What the biology does while you stop feeling it

Subjective habituation. Kirschbaum 1995 (Psychosom Med 57:468): across 5 daily Trier stress sessions, ~65% of healthy adults habituate — their cortisol and their subjective stress ratings decline. But that is only half the story.

Biological dissociation. Schommer, Hellhammer & Kirschbaum 2003 (Psychosom Med 65:450, N=65, 3 TSST sessions 4 weeks apart): salivary cortisol, plasma cortisol, ACTH and heart rate all decrease significantly across sessions (F>5.8, p<0.01). Norepinephrine and epinephrine do not. The HPA axis habituates; the sympathetic nervous system keeps firing at full strength every single time.

Mechanism (rodent). Radley et al. 2004 (Neuroscience 125:1): 21 days of restraint stress → −20% apical dendritic length and −17% branch number in mPFC pyramidal neurons. Radley 2005 (Exp Neurol 196:199): the atrophy reverses after 3 weeks of recovery.

Open-access links: Schommer 2003 PubMed · Kirschbaum 1995 PubMed · Radley 2004 PubMed
Kahneman — "System 2 drops out first." The deliberative, long-horizon, rule-based mode is the most metabolically expensive part of cognition and the first to fail under catecholamine load. What a PM feels as "I'm used to this now, it's just the job" is often System 2 offline, System 1 driving — and Schommer says the sympathetic half of the stress response never stopped.
Why this matters for investment. A PM three months into a sustained drawdown may truthfully report "I've adapted." Their HPA axis has habituated — they are telling the truth about their subjective experience. Their sympathetic nervous system, DLPFC–parietal connectivity, and working memory are telling a different story. Process design has to account for this: the cooling-off protocol, the independent review, the pre-committed horizon — all have to be installed before the cortisol is flowing, because self-assessment stops being reliable after the first two weeks.
Science · Time horizon compression · Prefrontal cortex 02 / 05

Under stress, time horizons shorten — and that alone mechanically amplifies risk aversion.

The prefrontal cortex holds the representations that let you plan far ahead. Knock it out and intertemporal choice collapses toward the near: 15 minutes of 1-Hz TMS to left dlPFC makes subjects measurably more impulsive (Figner 2010 Nat Neurosci). Stress — via cortisol and catecholamines — produces the same functional offline state (Arnsten 2009; Cornelisse 2018 pharmacological replication). The financial consequence of horizon compression is not subtle: in Thaler, Tversky, Kahneman & Schwartz's 1997 experiment, shortening the feedback frequency from yearly to monthly nearly doubled allocation to the safe asset — a 29-point swing from an identical information set.
Causal: knock out left dlPFC with TMS → impulsive choice rises.
Figner, Knoch, Johnson, Krosch, Lisanby, Fehr & Weber 2010, Nat Neurosci 13(5):538–539 · Figure 1 · N=52, 1-Hz rTMS to left vs right lateral PFC vs sham; effect in panel (a) immediately post-TMS is the headline · nature.com/articles/nn.2516
Y-axis: proportion of patient (larger-later) choices. X-axis: relative difference between sooner-smaller and later-larger options. Panel (a): immediately after rTMS to left dlPFC, the dark "Left rTMS" curve sits ~12-15 pp below Sham and Right rTMS across the 10–30% range. (b) 30 min post-TMS: effect dissipates. (c,d) "not-now" control trials: no group separation — effect is specific to self-control demands.
Shorter feedback horizon → nearly double the allocation to the safe asset, from identical information.
Thaler, Tversky, Kahneman & Schwartz 1997, QJE 112(2):647–661 · Figure I (p. 655) · N=80, experimental manipulation of evaluation-period length · faculty.chicagobooth.edu
Histograms of final bond-fund allocation by feedback-frequency condition. Means (Table I): Monthly 59.1% in bonds · Yearly 30.4% · 5-Yearly 33.8% · Inflated-Monthly 27.6%. Subjects who see monthly P&L allocate almost twice as much to the safe asset as subjects who see 5-yearly P&L — from the same return-generating process.
The Kahneman-Thaler mechanism — myopic loss aversion. With λ ≈ 2.25, losses are felt ~2.25× as intensely as equivalent gains. At a 1-month horizon, risky-asset returns are negative ~1 month in 3. At a 10-year horizon, cumulative losses are rare. Compress the evaluation horizon and effective risk aversion mechanically rises — without any change in the analyst's true preferences or information.
Why this matters for investment. The 2022 rates drawdown ran 9 months. Our analyst-note corpus shows written time horizons compressing across that window — a textual signature of the same PFC-offline state the Figner TMS experiment creates causally. A PM whose effective evaluation horizon has drifted from 3 years to 3 months isn't making a worse forecast — they are running their existing forecast through a mechanically higher risk-aversion parameter, and the output reads as a de-risking decision. The remedy is process — pre-committed horizon declarations, delayed P&L aggregation, scheduled re-allocation windows insulated from intra-cycle cortisol.
Supporting mechanism: Cornelisse et al. 2018 Psychoneuroendocrinology 88:161–68 (acute hydrocortisone → steeper discounting at 15 min, gone by 195 min); Haushofer & Fehr 2014 Science 344:862–67 · Figure 2 (four-panel synthesis of stress-induced risk & time preferences); Benartzi & Thaler 1995 QJE 110:73–92 · Fig 1 Panel D (implied equity-premium-consistent evaluation horizon ≈ 11–12 months).
Science · Overconfidence · Overprecision + stress-impaired metacognition 03 / 05

We are overconfident — and stress silences the brain region that would notice.

Overconfidence in expert forecasts is not a quirk of amateurs. The specific, training-resistant flavor is overprecision: confidence intervals too narrow for the actual variability of the world (Moore & Healy 2008). Top CFOs asked to bracket next year's S&P 500 with an 80% interval contain the true outcome 36% of the time on average — and nearly never in a crisis (Ben-David, Graham & Harvey 2013). Stress makes it worse via a precise mechanism: first-order accuracy and raw confidence are both unchanged, but metacognitive accuracy — the ability to know which of your own judgments is correct — collapses (Reyes 2015).
What CFOs said was an 80% confidence interval on the S&P 500 contained the truth only 36% of the time — and near-zero in 2008.
Ben-David, Graham & Harvey 2013, QJE 128(4):1547–1584 · Figure 1 (NBER w16215 working-paper version, p. 34) · 13,300+ CFO return-distribution forecasts over 37 quarterly Duke/CFO surveys, 2001–2011 · nber.org/papers/w16215
Per-quarter % of CFOs whose realized S&P 500 return fell inside their own 80% CI. Dashed line = 80% target. Bars almost never reach the target; many quarters near 0%. CFO-stated CI width: 14.5 pp. Empirical 10th–90th percentile spread: 42.2 pp. Their intervals are roughly a third of the width the data demand.
Under acute stress: accuracy unchanged, confidence unchanged — but metacognitive discrimination (knowing you're wrong) collapses.
Reyes, Silva, Jaramillo, Rehbein & Sackur 2015, PLOS ONE 10(7):e0132320 · Figure 3 · N=27, Trier Social Stress Test + perceptual discrimination task with confidence ratings · journals.plos.org/plosone
A. Anxiety rose with cortisol (manipulation check). B. Accuracy (%) and reaction time flat across stress groups. C. Type-2 ROC: p(confidence | correct) vs p(confidence | incorrect) — more bowed = better metacognition. Curves collapse toward diagonal with stress; AUC inset: Low 0.78 → Med 0.73 → High 0.68. D. Cortisol × metacognitive AUC: r²=0.34, p<0.001.
Kahneman's "illusion of validity" — the subjective sense of knowing is separable from any objective track record. Experts keep the feeling even when the feedback loop is long or noisy enough to never refute them. The antidote isn't less confidence — Cremers-Petajisto and Cohen-Polk-Silli show conviction from skilled managers carries real signal. The antidote is calibration: track each analyst's Brier score, size against calibration, not against raw conviction.
Why this matters — and why it ties back to stress. Overconfidence is the persistent baseline. Stress amplifies it by the exact pathway Slides 01 and 02 characterized: a depleted PFC cannot properly represent its own uncertainty, so the confidence number keeps coming out high while calibration behind it evaporates. Behavioral signature: unchanged conviction in increasingly noisy forecasts — the failure mode hardest to detect from the output alone. The BDGH result replicates out-of-sample (Boutros, Ben-David, Graham, Harvey & Makridis 2020, NBER w28010); it is a structural feature of the profession, not a sample artifact.
Supporting refs: Moore & Healy 2008 Psych Rev 115:502–517 (overprecision is the training-resistant subtype); Barber & Odean 2000 JF 55:773–806 · Figure 1 (highest-turnover quintile underperformed market by 650 bp annually — 65,000 households); Odean 1999 AER 89:1279–98 (stocks sold outperformed stocks bought by 3.2 pp at 1 year, gross of costs); Kahneman & Klein 2009 Am Psychologist 64:515–26 (conditions under which expert confidence is or is not informative); Fleming et al. 2010 Science 329:1541–43 · Figure 3B (aPFC gray-matter volume scatters with metacognitive accuracy).
Science · Extended stress · The biology keeps running, and it remodels the brain 04 / 05

When stress lasts weeks, biology keeps running while subjective awareness fades — and the brain is quietly remodeled.

Four figures, two pairs. Pair 1: your biology responds to stress every day, and the damage accumulates — whether or not you feel it. Real London bond traders' daily cortisol tracked Bund implied volatility with r²=0.86 (Coates & Herbert 2008 PNAS). Chronic caregivers show telomeres ~550 bp shorter than matched controls — the equivalent of a decade of extra biological aging (Epel et al. 2004 PNAS). Pair 2: the brain itself is structurally rewired. PFC pyramidal neurons lose dendrites under chronic stress (McEwen & Morrison 2013 Neuron); stressed animals shift from flexible goal-directed choice to rigid habit — even when the reward is explicitly devalued (Dias-Ferreira et al. 2009 Science).

Pair 2
The brain is structurally remodeled — and behavior shifts to habit
Chronic stress retracts dendrites in the PFC and expands them in the OFC — partial recovery with rest.
McEwen & Morrison 2013, Neuron 79(1):16–29 · Figure 2 · schematic of rodent-model consensus findings in mPFC / OFC pyramidal neurons under chronic restraint stress · pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3753223
Top: baseline pyramidal neuron. Middle: chronic stress retracts dendrites in prelimbic / anterior cingulate PFC (left) and expands them in orbitofrontal cortex (right). Bottom: infralimbic PFC shows retraction that partially reverses after a rest period. The architecture that supports top-down planning is physically smaller under extended stress.
Chronic stress shifts choice from goal-directed to habitual. The reward changed; the stressed animals kept pressing.
Dias-Ferreira, Sousa, Melo, Morgado, Mesquita, Cerqueira, Costa & Sousa 2009, Science 325(5940):621–625 · Figure 1 · rats, 21-day chronic unpredictable stress · science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1171203
A. Training: both groups learn to press. B–D. Devaluation test: control animals reduce pressing when the outcome is devalued (goal-directed updating). Stressed animals keep pressing — behavior is now habitual, insensitive to the actual value of the outcome. The same rigidity a PM shows when they keep running a thesis that no longer fits.
Kahneman — the shift is from slow, effortful, updating System 2 to fast, automatic, habitual System 1. Dias-Ferreira gives you the molecular and cellular story behind that shift: under multi-week stress the goal-directed striatal circuit (dorsomedial) atrophies and the habit circuit (dorsolateral) hypertrophies. The analyst who at Month 1 ran through counterfactuals and at Month 4 just "goes with what's worked" hasn't lost discipline — their frontostriatal wiring is literally different.
Why this matters for investment. This is the slide that answers "why can't we just tough it out and stay rational during drawdowns." Because toughing it out is the problem. The biology responds whether or not you consciously register it (Coates), the damage accumulates longitudinally (Epel), the PFC is physically smaller under extended stress (McEwen-Morrison), and the behavior it outputs shifts to habit and away from goal-directed updating (Dias-Ferreira). Every one of these has a direct analog on a trading desk during a prolonged drawdown. The process-level remedy on Slide A — cooling-off windows, pre-committed horizons, independent review — is the only intervention robust to all four pathways.
Science · Brain mechanism · Time-course and circuit-level takeover 05 / 05

What's actually happening in your brain: a neurochemical symphony on a staircase of timescales — and a hostile takeover of the control circuit.

The stress response is not one signal — it is an overlapping sequence of neurochemicals acting on time-scales from milliseconds to months (Joëls & Baram 2009 Nat Rev Neurosci). Monoamines (noradrenaline, dopamine) hit synapses within seconds of a stressor. CRH and other peptides follow within minutes. Corticosteroids (cortisol) bind non-genomic receptors within 15–30 minutes and genomic receptors over hours-to-days. The cumulative functional effect: under stress the PFC is chemically pushed offline while primitive circuits (amygdala, basal ganglia, brainstem LC) take over behavior (Arnsten 2015 Nat Neurosci).
The stress response unfolds on multiple timescales — seconds to months, with mediators that layer onto each other.
Joëls & Baram 2009, Nat Rev Neurosci 10:459–466 · Box 1 / Fig 4 · review of the neuroendocrinology of the stress response · pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2844123
Concentric rings from t=0 outward: monoamines (noradrenaline, dopamine) act within ms–min; CRH and other peptides within min–h; corticosteroids act non-genomically within min and genomically within hours; transcription factors and genomic/structural effects over hours–days–months. Each wave overlaps the last; extended stress keeps all of them activated.
Alert vs Stressed: the whole control architecture shifts from PFC top-down to amygdala + striatum + brainstem.
Arnsten 2015, Nat Neurosci 18(10):1376–1385 · Figure 1 · review of molecular mechanisms of stress-induced PFC dysfunction · pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4816215
Left (Alert): PFC sends green top-down control arrows to thought, action, and emotion circuits. Right (Stressed): PFC fades; red arrows now run from sensory cortex, amygdala, basal ganglia, PAG and locus coeruleus — reflexive / habitual responding driven by more primitive circuits. Behavior becomes automatic, emotional, and threat-biased; deliberation drops out.
Kahneman — the dual-systems framework maps one-to-one onto this picture. Alert brain = System 2 available; Stressed brain = System 2 pushed offline, System 1 in command. The neuroscience doesn't override Kahneman — it gives the mechanism and the chemistry behind what he described behaviorally 40 years ago. Each of the four biases we worry about in investment decisions (anchoring, availability, loss aversion, narrow framing) lives in the System 1 circuits these two diagrams name.
The takeaway across Slides 01–05. Every behavioral finding in the rest of this deck is downstream of these two diagrams. Horizon compression (Slide 02) is a direct read-out of the PFC-offline state on the right-hand brain. Overconfidence + failed metacognition (Slide 03) is a read-out of the same state — the cortical circuits that monitor one's own uncertainty sit in the faded regions. The extended-stress impacts (Slide 04) are the outer ring of the Joëls-Baram diagram playing out in a trader's brain over weeks. The pathway is well-characterized. The remaining question is entirely about process design around it.
