Eight days of elevated cortisol cut risk-taking by 44%.

A controlled experiment dosed healthy adults with hydrocortisone for 8 days, matching the cortisol elevation seen in real traders during a market downturn. Risk-taking collapsed.
Utility curvature α (lower = more risk-averse)
Certainty equivalent for a £100 coin-flip lottery
What it means. Sustained cortisol elevation reshapes how the brain values uncertain payoffs — the same risky lottery becomes substantially less attractive. The effect is causal: it appears under pharmacological dosing in healthy volunteers, not just in traders during stress.
Source: Kandasamy et al. 2014, PNAS 111(9):3608–13 · "Cortisol shifts financial risk preferences" · N = 36 healthy adults (20 M / 16 F), 8-day weight-adjusted hydrocortisone (3×/day) elevated cortisol 69%; α placebo 0.50 → cortisol 0.35 (P = 0.022); CE £25 → £14 (−44%) · pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1317908111
Stress & decision-making · Capital Group RQS 02 / 04

Too much stress chemistry breaks the prefrontal cortex's deliberative function.

The prefrontal cortex — the deliberation engine — operates within a narrow window of arousal. Push catecholamines (norepinephrine, dopamine) too low or too high and PFC working memory degrades. Empirical "inverted-U" demonstrated in monkey single-neuron recordings and decades of pharmacological work.
What it means. Acute stress drives catecholamines past the optimal zone. PFC working memory drops. Decisions that need deliberation — multi-step planning, considering counterfactuals, holding future scenarios in mind — degrade. Lower-order circuits (amygdala, striatum) take over the steering. Causal evidence: β-blockers (propranolol) restore PFC function under stress; α2A-adrenergic agonists (guanfacine) shift the curve toward higher tolerance.
Sources: Arnsten 2009, Nat Rev Neurosci 10:410–22 "Stress signalling pathways that impair prefrontal cortex structure and function" · nature.com/articles/nrn2648 · Original empirical inverted-U in monkey PFC: Vijayraghavan, Wang, Birnbaum, Williams, Arnsten 2007, Nat Neurosci 10:376–84 "Inverted-U dopamine D1 receptor actions on prefrontal neurons engaged in working memory" · nature.com/articles/nn1846 · Lab figure: medicine.yale.edu/lab/arnsten/research/invertedu
Stress & decision-making · Capital Group RQS 03 / 04

Acute cortisol shifts choices toward smaller, sooner rewards.

A double-blind hydrocortisone challenge in healthy adults: 15 minutes after dosing, subjects became markedly more impulsive in intertemporal choice — preferring small immediate payoffs to larger delayed ones. The effect is acute and time-limited.
Acute · 15 min post-cortisol
Strongly increased preference for the small, soon reward over the larger, delayed reward. Effect is fast — present at 15 min, absent at 195 min — consistent with non-genomic glucocorticoid signaling.
Riis-Vestergaard / Cornelisse et al. 2018, Psychoneuroendocrinology · 10 mg hydrocortisone vs placebo, double-blind
Chronic · 8-day cortisol elevation
Risk premium collapses 44%; utility curvature flattens. Same chronic cortisol regimen that mirrors the stress profile of traders during downturns.
Kandasamy et al. 2014, PNAS
Mechanism · why shorter horizons bite
Myopic loss aversion. When the evaluation horizon shortens, loss aversion (λ ≈ 2.25) mechanically dominates expected returns — explaining the equity premium puzzle and why high-frequency re-evaluation under stress amplifies risk-aversion.
Benartzi & Thaler 1995, QJE
Honest caveat
Acute psychosocial-stress effects on delay discounting are more variable across studies — a 2024 meta-analysis found no consistent effect for laboratory psychosocial stressors. The cleanest causal evidence is for pharmacological cortisol (Cornelisse 2018) and chronic cortisol elevation (Kandasamy 2014).
Sources: Cornelisse et al. 2018, Psychoneuroendocrinology 88:161–168 · Kandasamy et al. 2014, PNAS · Benartzi & Thaler 1995, QJE 110(1):73–92.

Acute stress reconfigures the whole brain: salience network up, executive network down.

In a placebo-controlled fMRI study, exposing healthy adults to a fear-related acute stressor caused a brain-wide network shift. The "salience hijack" is mediated by noradrenergic signaling — propranolol blocks it, the cortisol blocker metyrapone does not.
Primary source · open access via Donders / Radboud
"Stress-Related Noradrenergic Activity Prompts Large-Scale Neural Network Reconfiguration"
Hermans, van Marle, Ossewaarde, Henckens, Qin, van Kesteren, Schoots, Cousijn, Rijpkema, Oostenveld, Fernández · Science 334(6059):1151–53 · 25 Nov 2011
Paper: science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1209603
Open-access PDF (figures included): pdodds.w3.uvm.edu/files/papers/others/2011/hermans2011a.pdf
Figures-only view: science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1209603 (Fig. 2)
What goes UP under stress
Salience network — anterior insula, dorsal anterior cingulate, amygdala, hypothalamus, midbrain. Functional connectivity strengthens with stress-response magnitude.
What goes DOWN under stress
Executive control network — dorsolateral PFC, lateral parietal cortex. Top-down control weakens; deliberation is suppressed in favor of vigilance.
Noradrenaline drives it
Propranolol BLOCKS the reconfiguration. A β-adrenergic blocker prevents the stress-induced network shift — implicating the locus-coeruleus noradrenergic system as the proximal cause.
Cortisol does NOT
Metyrapone (cortisol synthesis inhibitor) does NOT block the fast salience-network shift. Cortisol drives a slower, recovery-phase reconfiguration on a different time-scale.
